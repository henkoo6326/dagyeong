The shape of attention

On making things, slowly.

An object begins before its shape.

It begins with a question. How will this feel in a hand? Where will it sit when it is not being used? What will make someone reach for it again tomorrow? At DAGYEONG, these ordinary questions are the most useful ones.

The small decisions matter.

A rim is only a few millimetres of an object, but it is often the part we notice first. The hand meets it when we lift a cup; the lip meets it when we drink. Attention to this small edge can change the whole experience.

Room for a trace.

We are drawn to surfaces that reveal something about their making. A soft turn in clay, a line in wood, a slight change in the way a glaze catches light. Variation can give an object presence without asking it to become decoration.

Useful, before anything else.

The aim is not to make everyday life precious. It is to make useful things worth noticing. Objects become familiar through repeated use, gathering meaning in the ordinary moments they accompany.
