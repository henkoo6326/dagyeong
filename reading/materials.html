A language of materials.

A surface holds more than a colour. It carries the trace of how something was formed — and the possibility of how it will be used.

Clay

The hand leaves a trace.

We are interested in the meeting of precision and variation. The curve of a rim. The weight of a bowl. A surface that changes as the light moves across it.

Wood

Warmth, without ornament.

Grain gives a surface its own rhythm. We look for simple construction and edges that feel right to the hand, allowing the material to do the talking.

Leaf

Small things, closely observed.

Before the water, there is the leaf. Its shape and scent offer a way into the cup. We approach tea through attention and curiosity, leaving room for personal taste.

From process
to presence.

Our aim is to make the thinking behind an object visible without making the object complicated. A thoughtful material, a useful shape, a reason to keep reaching for it.
